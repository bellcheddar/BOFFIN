🧬 BOFFIN

A protein language model in your pocket: paste a sequence, and an iPhone tells you where the domain starts, which mutations it will tolerate, what family it belongs to and what has already been crystallised, then shows you the structure. No cloud, no account, no signal required.

🌐 Websitemarcdeller.com ✉️ Contactmarc@marcdeller.com 🐙 GitHubbellcheddar/Boffin

BOFFIN (Boundary, Order, Fitness and Family INference) is a native iPhone and iPad app that runs a protein language model on Apple's Neural Engine, entirely on the device, and pairs it with a full interactive structure viewer intended as a mobile equivalent of PyMOL.

Why it matters: the questions a structural biologist asks first about a new protein (where does the ordered part begin and end, which residues can I mutate, what family is this, has anyone solved something like it) currently mean a laptop, a queue on a cluster, or a web server that wants a copy of your sequence. BOFFIN answers all of them from a single pass of a neural network that finishes in about the time it takes to lift your thumb, on a phone, in aeroplane mode, with the sequence never leaving your hand. It is useful for: designing a construct at the bench before you order the gene, sanity-checking a variant on the train, triaging a set of homologs between meetings, and presenting a structure from an iPad without carrying a laptop into the room.

Research use only. No clinical, diagnostic or therapeutic claims.

🧠 What a protein language model knows

A protein language model is trained the way a text model is: show it hundreds of millions of natural protein sequences with residues hidden, and ask it to guess what was removed. To do that well it has to learn what evolution has already learned. Which positions never change because the fold collapses without them. Which pairs of residues always change together because they touch each other in space. Which stretches are so tolerant of substitution that they are probably not folded at all.

BOFFIN uses ESM-2 (Lin et al., Science 2023), in its 35-million-parameter size. That is deliberately not the largest one. The 15-billion-parameter version is a few points better on most benchmarks and will not run on a phone at all, and the practical question is not which model wins a leaderboard but which model answers a bench question before you lose interest in the answer.

What comes out of the model is not a prediction. It is a representation: a 480-number description of every residue, and a matrix of how surprised the model is by the residue that is actually there. Everything BOFFIN shows you is read out of those two things.

⚡ Why the Neural Engine

Every Apple silicon chip carries three processors that can do this arithmetic: the CPU, the GPU, and the Neural Engine (ANE), a block designed for nothing but neural network inference. The ANE is far faster per watt than the other two, which on a phone is the whole game: it is the difference between an answer and a warm handset with a flat battery.

Getting a model onto it is not a switch you flip. The ANE is fixed-function hardware with strong opinions, and a model converted the obvious way runs almost entirely on the GPU while reporting success. BOFFIN's conversion follows Apple's published tensor layout for transformers from the outset, and the result is measured rather than assumed:

98.8% of the model's operations run on the Neural Engine (746 of 755), verified by asking Core ML for its own compute plan rather than by watching a graph in a profiler.

Two things were learned making that true, and both are the sort of thing that silently costs you the feature:

  • The ANE is fp16 hardware. Ask for the full 32-bit precision a desktop uses and residency is zero: everything falls back. The numerical agreement with the reference implementation therefore has to be judged in relative terms, which it passes: cosine similarity 0.99997, and the mutation scores agree with the reference at a Spearman correlation of 0.99998.
  • Attention with rotary position encoding caches itself by sequence length. A naively traced model freezes that cache at whatever length happened to be traced, and then quietly returns nonsense for every other protein. BOFFIN converts a set of length buckets and stitches longer sequences from overlapping tiles.

One pass, four answers

The expensive step is running the model. So BOFFIN runs it once, and every feature in the app is a different read-out of that same pass:

What is read What it becomes
The per-residue description Disorder, secondary structure, transmembrane helices, domain boundaries
How surprised the model is at each position A full mutation-tolerance map, all 20 amino acids at every residue
The average over the whole chain Family classification, and similarity search against every protein in the PDB

This is why the app can be fast on a phone rather than merely possible on one. Describing a 300-residue protein takes about 31 ms, and searching all 72,421 proteins in the PDB for its nearest relatives takes 5.4 ms. Both figures are measured on the development Mac against phone budgets of 250 ms and 100 ms respectively; measuring them on the device itself is still outstanding, and is on the list below rather than quietly assumed.

🔬 What it does

Tab What you get
Order Where the protein is folded and where it is not: disorder, secondary structure, transmembrane helices, signal peptide, low complexity. Plus molecular weight, isoelectric point, extinction coefficient and hydropathy over any stretch you select
Fitness A heat map of every possible point mutation, scored by how much the model dislikes it. Sequence logos, a mutation basket, and CSV export for the ones you want to order
Family What family this is, with a confidence that has been checked against how often it is actually right. Kinase pocket numbering and GPCR generic numbering mapped onto your sequence. Motif annotation. Similarity search over the whole PDB
Boundary Construct design: truncations ranked, tags planned, proteases checked, and codon-optimised DNA ready for primers. Refusal is a valid answer, with the reason given
Structure A full Mol*-based viewer: representations, colour themes, a PyMOL-style selection language, measurements, interaction diagrams, saved scenes and a presentation mode. Open a .pml from the desktop, or send one back

One ruler, everything on it

Disorder, secondary structure, transmembrane spans, mutation tolerance, motifs and structural contacts are all just numbers along the sequence. BOFFIN stacks them on a single scrollable ruler, so you read them against each other rather than flipping between five views. The tabs are filters over that one ruler, not five separate tools.

The corresponding hazard is taken seriously: a track that is out of register with its sequence does not crash, it draws a perfectly convincing picture of the wrong protein. Every track is checked against its sequence at the moment it is made.

🙅 What it will not tell you

An app that only advertises what it is good at is a sales brochure. These are on the screen, not just in this file.

  • The family classifier is closed set. It knows 100 families and must answer with one of them. Ubiquitin, which is not among them, is confidently called something else at 79.7%. No confidence threshold catches this, because the model genuinely is confident, so the limitation is printed on every call rather than hidden behind a number.
  • Disorder prediction is weakest exactly where you need it most. On sequences with no close relative in the PDB it currently underperforms not using a language model at all.
  • A predicted structure is not a structure. An experimental temperature factor and an AlphaFold confidence occupy the same field of the same file format and mean opposite things. BOFFIN carries which one it has through the entire app and captions every predicted model accordingly.
  • Interaction analysis rests on assumptions about hydrogens. Most crystal structures do not contain any, so hydrogen bonds are inferred from geometry alone. The specific assumptions travel inside the exported data, above the results, rather than as a footnote nobody reads.
  • The deposited coordinates are often not the molecule. A dimer deposited as one chain looks like a monomer until the biological assembly is built. The viewer says which one you are looking at.

🔒 Everything stays on the device

There is no inference server, no account, and no telemetry. The privacy manifest declares that BOFFIN collects nothing, and not "nothing identifiable": nothing. Structure files can be fetched from the PDB and AlphaFold if you ask for them, and that is the only network traffic the app generates. An unpublished sequence pasted into BOFFIN never leaves the phone, which is the difference between using it on a real project and using it on a demo.

⚖️ Where the data comes from

Every model, dataset and library is recorded with its licence, read at source and dated, in Docs/ATTRIBUTIONS.md, and credited in the app whether or not the licence asks for it.

Two consequences worth stating plainly. PLIP is GPL v2, so BOFFIN's interaction analysis is a clean-room implementation of the published geometric criteria and PLIP's code is never linked, ported or consulted. And a licence question turned out to be a science question: the transmembrane predictor could not use the standard training sets because they are not redistributable, so the labels were rebuilt from Swiss-Prot's own curated annotations, which are CC BY 4.0. The replacement is not a compromise, it is where the curators put the annotation in the first place.

Where a source states no terms at all, it is used for non-commercial research and the position is recorded as a risk rather than dressed up as a permission (decision of 2026-08-25).

🔧 For developers

BOFFIN.xcodeproj is a plain Xcode project committed to the repository: clone it, open it, build it, with no generation step. The code sits in seven local Swift packages whose dependency graph is enforced by a script rather than by convention. Build, test and architecture detail is in CLAUDE.md, the full engineering plan in Docs/BOFFIN_BUILD_PLAN.md, and every performance number in Docs/perf-log.md.

Test fixtures are real structures chosen so that each one can fail differently: ubiquitin, CDK2 with ATP, the β2-adrenergic receptor, PETase, α-synuclein by NMR, a 70S ribosome, a selenomethionine structure, and eight deliberately malformed inputs. Where scientific correctness is at stake, the test is written from the published definition before the implementation, because a numbering scheme that is silently wrong is worse than one that is missing: it will be believed.

✅ To Do

The roadmap, in dependency order. Each phase ends with tests green, performance measured and a build you can hold. Completed items keep their reasoning, because the list doubles as the project record. Suggestions welcome.

  • ✅ Foundations. Repository, module structure with a mechanically enforced dependency rule, continuous integration, the shared residue ruler, and the golden fixture set with provenance and checksums. The rule-checker is deliberately tested against planted violations, because a checker that has never failed is not a checker
  • ✅ Chose a plain Xcode project over a generator. Trialled and reversed on 2026-08-24. The accepted cost is that project changes merge badly; the gain is that a clone opens and builds with no extra toolchain
  • ✅ The sequence spine. Sequence parsing that reports what it changed instead of silently fixing it, the analytical properties every construct paper quotes, and a virtualised ruler that stays smooth on a phone. One real correctness trap found: the widely copied reference implementation of isoelectric point searches only pH 4.05 to 12 and returns the boundary for anything outside it, so a very acidic peptide reads exactly 4.05. BOFFIN searches 0 to 14 and reads 3.49
  • ✅ The machine learning core. The premise proven: 98.8% of operations on the Neural Engine, parity with the reference implementation on every length bucket, and a cross-language test so a tokenising mistake on the Swift side cannot hide behind its own consistency
  • ✅ Secondary structure and disorder. Trained from scratch, because the published heads cannot be adapted: they are built on a different, much larger backbone, their architectures would forfeit the Neural Engine entirely, and they are not redistributable. Measured against the published baselines including the no-language-model floor, which is the comparison that says whether the model is contributing anything
  • ⬜ Improve disorder on novel folds. Currently below the no-language-model floor on CASP12. Worth trying: predicting solvent accessibility jointly, more capacity in the head, or a reference set with structure-derived negatives
  • ✅ Transmembrane spans and signal peptides. The blocker was a licence, not an algorithm, and the answer was to go back to the curators: Swiss-Prot annotates both directly and is CC BY 4.0. Accepted on a structure rather than an average: the β2-adrenergic receptor shows exactly seven spans, ubiquitin none, PETase none. One appealing hypothesis died on the way: fragmented helix predictions look like they want short gaps merged, and merging them fuses genuinely adjacent helices at every gap size tested
  • ✅ Removed a licence-encumbered dependency from the numbering path. The mapping between a PDB entry and a UniProt sequence came from a source that states no terms, and the obvious substitute inherits the same provenance. The way out was a different source, not a different supplier: the information is inside every structure file, and the PDB archive is public domain. What is lost is stated rather than glossed, since the in-file version is the depositor's alignment rather than a curated correction of it
  • ✅ Settled the unstated-terms question. Marc's decision, 2026-08-25: sources that state no terms are used for non-commercial research, attributed regardless. Silence is not a permissive licence, so this is recorded as a risk position
  • ⬜ Revisit the licence position before any commercial release. A different decision with a different risk profile
  • ✅ The Fitness tab. Every point mutation scored, as a heat map and as sequence logos, with a basket for the ones you intend to make. Two scoring modes, each labelled with how it was produced, because the fast one is an approximation and saying so is cheaper than being caught. Found a bug that produced an entirely convincing heat map of garbage: the model's output buffer is padded rather than densely packed, so reading it by arithmetic instead of by its own strides shifts every row after the first
  • ⬜ Measure everything on a real device. Every performance figure so far comes from a development Mac, so the phone budgets are unverified rather than met. The pressing one is the full mutation scan, at 9.35 s per 300 residues on a Mac against a 6 s phone budget. Batching would recover about a third and the framework will not combine batching with variable-length input, so this needs device numbers, a different packaging, or an honest budget
  • ✅ Family motifs. Kinase and class A GPCR motifs annotated on the shared ruler, detected by sequence pattern plus ordering, because the HRD motif occurs by chance about once per 8,000 residues. Validated against CDK2's textbook numbering, and just as importantly, correctly refused on ubiquitin, a GPCR and PETase. The variants are handled because they were counted across all 521 human kinases rather than assumed
  • ✅ Canonical numbering. Alignment maps a pasted sequence onto the curated kinase-pocket and GPCR numbering tables, so the published position names land on your residues. Two corrections on the way: percent identity measured over aligned columns only is badly biased (ubiquitin scored 0.35 against a kinase pocket that way, on a short flattering overlap), and alignment score is the wrong instrument for deciding family membership, so numbering is gated on the motif detector instead
  • ✅ The family classifier. 97.8% top-1 across 100 families in under a megabyte. Confidence calibration was fitted and then deliberately not applied, because the held-out split said it made things worse: calibration is a correction for over-confidence, not a ritual
  • ⬜ Expand coverage beyond 100 families. Pfam has over 30,000, and the real fix for closed-set behaviour is coverage plus a genuine rejection mechanism. Distance to the nearest family centre was measured as a candidate and is too weak to separate a correct call from a confident mistake
  • ✅ Homolog search over the whole PDB. Every protein in the archive reduced to one vector, searched exhaustively in 5.4 ms. Approximate search was considered and rejected: at this size the exact answer is already fast, so approximation would buy nothing and pay in recall you cannot see going missing. Three defects found on the way, none of which raised an error: a truncated download left 7% of the archive in the index and quietly redefined "the best structure of this protein" as "the alphabetically first one"; ranking by resolution picks fragments, so a 90-residue tail was about to represent a GPCR; and a length ceiling had silently excluded EGFR along with 3,533 others
  • ✅ Whitened the search index, which was losing a quarter of its answers in silence. Protein embeddings are anisotropic: two unrelated proteins already score 0.848 similar, so everything that distinguishes a real hit from a stranger lives in the last two percent of the range, which is precisely what compressing to 8 bits destroys. Recall was 0.748 with no error and no symptom. Removing the shared directions took it to 0.966, and made the number on screen mean something as a side effect
  • ✅ Construct design. Truncations ranked against hard constraints that are enforced rather than scored, because a construct that bisects a catalytic motif is not a compromise, it is dead protein. Precedent from real PDB entries is translated into the user's own numbering. Tag planning refuses a protease whose recognition site occurs inside the construct, since TEV cutting the protein in half presents as proteolysis in the prep rather than as a design error
  • ✅ The construct card. Everything you would paste into a supplier's form, as plain text with a share sheet, and every number on it either measured or labelled a convention. One mismatch caught: the pH scale used was a parameter to the printer rather than a property of the result, so a card could print one provenance above the other scale's numbers
  • ✅ Primer-ready DNA. The codon usage table is computed from 4,317 coding sequences and 1.34 million codons of the E. coli reference genome rather than copied. The widely quoted published table for this organism rests on 14 genes: a fine sample of fourteen genes, and exactly the sort of artefact that acquires authority by being cited. A failing test bought one codon of lookahead, because a legal choice can strand the next residue into creating a restriction site
  • ⬜ Disulfide pairs as a hard constraint. A boundary can currently separate two cysteines that pair, because pairing cannot be read off a sequence. Needs the structure
  • ✅ The structure viewer. Mol* vendored offline with a pinned version, structure files parsed from the specification, and any ruler track paintable onto the structure. Ubiquitin loads with no network and Mol* counts 660 atoms, exactly what BOFFIN's own parser counts, which is two independent implementations agreeing. Nothing is ever interpolated into JavaScript, and a test proves it by passing a chain name that is an attempted injection and asserting it survives as data
  • ✅ Fetching, picking, and predictions labelled as predictions. Tap a residue and the app knows which one you meant, including in structures with more than one chain. PDB and AlphaFold fetch, with the AlphaFold model version pinned rather than "latest", because a silently changing model is a silently changing figure
  • ✅ Biological assemblies and NMR ensembles. The viewer lists what a structure declares, builds it, and says which you are looking at. Memory pressure releases the structure and keeps the page alive, so recovery is one command rather than a blank screen
  • ⬜ Surfaces, symmetry mates and figure export. High-resolution and transparent export for publication figures
  • ✅ A PyMOL-style selection language. Tokeniser, parser and evaluator. byres (polymer within 5 of organic) returns CDK2's ATP site, checked against structural facts rather than against the evaluator's own output. An unknown keyword names itself rather than quietly selecting everything or nothing, because an over-broad selection makes a figure that is wrong in a way nobody can see
  • ✅ Measurement. Distances, angles and torsions, with the sign of a torsion treated as the point rather than a detail: a viewer reporting the wrong sign is reporting the wrong conformation and both look equally plausible. Checked on constructed geometry and then on ubiquitin, where the peptide bond measures 1.33 Å and omega is trans. One hand-built test fixture was wrong, and the real-structure test passing beside it is what said so
  • ✅ .pml round-tripping. A scene deck built on a phone opens on a desktop. The import is the half that has to fail loudly, since a viewer that quietly ignores what it cannot represent draws a scene that is subtly not the one described. Selection expressions travel verbatim, which is the entire reason for adopting PyMOL's syntax rather than inventing one
  • ✅ Scene decks and presentation mode. Full-bleed presentation with presenter notes. Advancing stops at the end rather than wrapping, because finding slide one again reads as having lost your place, and it is deliberately not tap-to-advance: in front of a room you rotate the molecule far more often than you change slide
  • ⬜ Touch selection builder, external display and Pencil. A visual builder so the selection language is optional, a presenter view with the structure on the projector, and annotation anchored to the scene
  • ✅ Interaction analysis, clean-room. Hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, stacking, hydrophobic contacts and metal coordination from the published geometric criteria, in one tunable set of cutoffs. Where a criterion cannot honestly be applied it is not applied silently. One test tightens every cutoff and asserts strictly fewer contacts, because a criterion that is read and never used looks exactly like one that is used and generous
  • ✅ Interaction diagram, summary and export. Drawn on a circle rather than a force-directed layout, which would look more like the published figures and would move a residue between two renders of the same site, making the figures impossible to compare. Contact types differ by dash pattern as well as colour so the figure survives being printed in grey, and VoiceOver reads every one
  • ✅ Fixed two viewer commands that were doing nothing at all. The vendored build exports far fewer names at the top level than its source suggests, and code written against the missing ones does not throw: it evaluates to undefined and takes the fallback branch. Assembly listing returned empty forever and every pick reported the first atom rather than the one tapped, always plausibly. Every test passed throughout, because they checked a command was dispatched rather than that it did anything
  • ⬜ Verify assembly discovery against a structure known to declare one. Implemented and guarded, but the test asserting it was deleted rather than pinned to one viewer version
  • ⬜ The 3D interaction overlay. Dashed lines in the viewer. Built twice and removed twice: the second attempt measured 0 of 40 lines drawn, with the viewer reporting success and no error. The counter that caught it exists precisely because an overlay drawing nothing looks identical to one with nothing to draw
  • ✅ Opening files and Shortcuts. A sequence shared from Mail, Files or AirDrop opens and analyses. Two Shortcuts actions run without bringing the app forward, and expose only the analysis that needs no model, because a Shortcut that sometimes fails because an asset was not downloaded is worse than one that never offered
  • ⬜ Extensions and sync. Share extension, Quick Look preview, CloudKit sync, Handoff, widget and Live Activity
  • ✅ Audio Graphs. Continuous tracks carry a chart description, so a VoiceOver user hears hydropathy or a mutation-tolerance profile rather than being read a summary of it. A track is a shape, and the question a shape answers is where does it change. Gaps stay gaps: only continuous tracks are sonified, because a tone where there is no measurement is a lie in a new medium
  • ✅ Privacy manifest and acknowledgements. The manifest declares that BOFFIN collects nothing. Every source is credited whether or not its licence asks, and the terms column reads "None stated" in orange where that is true, because rounding an unstated licence up on an acknowledgements screen would mislead exactly where a reader has gone looking for the truth
  • ✅ Answered whether the dataset curation was load-bearing. Heads trained on labels taken straight from PDB entries (5,012 chains, 1.48 million residues, split so the score cannot be memorisation) land inside the range of the heads trained on the curated versions of the same underlying data. Not a like-for-like comparison and not quoted as one. The licence question, since decided separately, was never a question about quality
  • ✅ Onboarding, and empty states that act. A one-time welcome sheet saying the three things a new user needs, in that order: nothing leaves the device, one pass gives four answers, and what it gets wrong. The third is the point, because a welcome screen that only sells is how someone comes to trust the closed-set classifier the first time it is confidently wrong. Three tabs previously said "load a sequence in the Order tab first" and offered no way to do it, which is a dead end wearing the clothes of an explanation; each now carries the input sheet and both examples. The suppression flag the UI tests use has its own guard: one test deliberately launches without it, since fourteen green tests would say nothing about whether onboarding exists at all
  • ✅ Dynamic Type audit. Ten fixed point sizes, classified on a distinction a protein app has to make: text laid out as text must scale, text drawn inside a canvas must not, because a residue label has to fit the column it labels and enlarging it to 310% makes the heat map unreadable rather than accessible. Nine were text and are fixed, all of them 9 or 10 points and so below the legibility floor even at the default setting. One defect fell out: Typography.sequence claimed in its own doc comment to scale with Dynamic Type and never did, while the test checked only the floor, so nothing could contradict the half that did not exist
  • ✅ Error states, and the conflation underneath them. Five places handed the user a raw Core ML error string, which is a good diagnostic and not a message. Failures now carry what did not happen, what to do about it, and the raw text kept verbatim behind a disclosure. Cancellation is never reported as a failure, being offline says what still works, and recovery advice is absent rather than invented. Underneath, three state enums each had one case carrying both "this asset was never downloaded" and "this operation failed", so a corrupt index displayed as a download in progress
  • ⬜ TestFlight. Needs the Apple Developer account, signing identities and an App Store Connect record
  • ⬜ Native renderer and augmented reality. A Metal renderer benchmarked against Mol* rather than assumed to beat it, and RealityKit presentation. Gated on measured performance, not preference
  • ⬜ Choose a licence. Constrained by the copyleft dependency that is already avoided by clean-room implementation, and by what an App Store release permits
  • ⬜ Confirm three fixture caveats. Whether the GPCR tests should assert against the fusion construct or the canonical sequence, whether the interaction profiler wants a drug-like inhibitor rather than ATP, and whether the ribosome should be fetched on demand to lighten the repository

🚫 Out of scope for v1

Structure prediction (no AlphaFold or Boltz running on the device), multiple sequence alignment generation, molecular dynamics, docking, and any cloud inference whatsoever. Prediction requests are handed off to BoltzMaker on the desktop instead.
