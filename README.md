**BOFFIN** (**B**oundary, **O**rder, **F**itness and **F**amily **IN**ference) is a native iPhone and iPad app that runs a protein language model on Apple's Neural Engine, entirely on the device, and pairs it with a full interactive structure viewer intended as a mobile equivalent of PyMOL.

Why it matters: the questions a structural biologist asks first about a new protein (where does the ordered part begin and end, which residues can I mutate, what family is this, has anyone solved something like it) currently mean a laptop, a queue on a cluster, or a web server that wants a copy of your sequence. BOFFIN answers all of them from a single pass of a neural network that finishes in about the time it takes to lift your thumb, on a phone, in aeroplane mode, with the sequence never leaving your hand. It is useful for: designing a construct at the bench before you order the gene, sanity-checking a variant on the train, triaging a set of homologs between meetings, and presenting a structure from an iPad without carrying a laptop into the room.

Research use only. No clinical, diagnostic or therapeutic claims.

## 🧠 What a protein language model knows

A protein language model is trained the way a text model is: show it hundreds of millions of natural protein sequences with residues hidden, and ask it to guess what was removed. To do that well it has to learn what evolution has already learned. Which positions never change because the fold collapses without them. Which pairs of residues always change together because they touch each other in space. Which stretches are so tolerant of substitution that they are probably not folded at all.

BOFFIN uses **ESM-2** (Lin *et al.*, *Science* 2023), in its 35-million-parameter size. That is deliberately not the largest one. The 15-billion-parameter version is a few points better on most benchmarks and will not run on a phone at all, and the practical question is not which model wins a leaderboard but which model answers a bench question before you lose interest in the answer.

What comes out of the model is not a prediction. It is a **representation**: a 480-number description of every residue, and a matrix of how surprised the model is by the residue that is actually there. Everything BOFFIN shows you is read out of those two things.

## ⚡ Why the Neural Engine

Every Apple silicon chip carries three processors that can do this arithmetic: the CPU, the GPU, and the **Neural Engine** (ANE), a block designed for nothing but neural network inference. The ANE is far faster per watt than the other two, which on a phone is the whole game: it is the difference between an answer and a warm handset with a flat battery.

Getting a model onto it is not a switch you flip. The ANE is fixed-function hardware with strong opinions, and a model converted the obvious way runs almost entirely on the GPU while reporting success. BOFFIN's conversion follows Apple's published tensor layout for transformers from the outset, and the result is measured rather than assumed:

> **98.8% of the model's operations run on the Neural Engine** (746 of 755), verified by asking Core ML for its own compute plan rather than by watching a graph in a profiler.

Two things were learned making that true, and both are the sort of thing that silently costs you the feature:

- **The ANE is fp16 hardware.** Ask for the full 32-bit precision a desktop uses and residency is *zero*: everything falls back. The numerical agreement with the reference implementation therefore has to be judged in relative terms, which it passes: cosine similarity 0.99997, and the mutation scores agree with the reference at a Spearman correlation of 0.99998.
- **Attention with rotary position encoding caches itself by sequence length.** A naively traced model freezes that cache at whatever length happened to be traced, and then quietly returns nonsense for every other protein. BOFFIN converts a set of length buckets and stitches longer sequences from overlapping tiles.

### One pass, four answers

The expensive step is running the model. So BOFFIN runs it **once**, and every feature in the app is a different read-out of that same pass:

| What is read | What it becomes |
|---|---|
| The per-residue description | Disorder, secondary structure, transmembrane helices, domain boundaries |
| How surprised the model is at each position | A full mutation-tolerance map, all 20 amino acids at every residue |
| The average over the whole chain | Family classification, and similarity search against every protein in the PDB |

This is why the app can be fast on a phone rather than merely possible on one. Describing a 300-residue protein takes about **31 ms**, and searching all 72,421 proteins in the PDB for its nearest relatives takes **5.4 ms**. Both figures are measured on the development Mac against phone budgets of 250 ms and 100 ms respectively; measuring them on the device itself is still outstanding, and is on the list below rather than quietly assumed.

## 🔬 What it does

| Tab | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Order** | Where the protein is folded and where it is not: disorder, secondary structure, transmembrane helices, signal peptide, low complexity. Plus molecular weight, isoelectric point, extinction coefficient and hydropathy over any stretch you select |
| **Fitness** | A heat map of every possible point mutation, scored by how much the model dislikes it. Sequence logos, a mutation basket, and CSV export for the ones you want to order |
| **Family** | What family this is, with a confidence that has been checked against how often it is actually right. Kinase pocket numbering and GPCR generic numbering mapped onto *your* sequence. Motif annotation. Similarity search over the whole PDB |
| **Boundary** | Construct design: truncations ranked, tags planned, proteases checked, and codon-optimised DNA ready for primers. Refusal is a valid answer, with the reason given |
| **Structure** | A full Mol\*-based viewer: representations, colour themes, a PyMOL-style selection language, measurements, interaction diagrams, saved scenes and a presentation mode. Open a `.pml` from the desktop, or send one back |

### One ruler, everything on it

Disorder, secondary structure, transmembrane spans, mutation tolerance, motifs and structural contacts are all just *numbers along the sequence*. BOFFIN stacks them on a single scrollable ruler, so you read them against each other rather than flipping between five views. The tabs are filters over that one ruler, not five separate tools.

The corresponding hazard is taken seriously: a track that is out of register with its sequence does not crash, it draws a perfectly convincing picture of the wrong protein. Every track is checked against its sequence at the moment it is made.

## 🙅 What it will not tell you

An app that only advertises what it is good at is a sales brochure. These are on the screen, not just in this file.

- **The family classifier is closed set.** It knows 500 Pfam families and must answer with one of them, so a protein from outside that set is assigned the nearest, confidently. A check on the head's own confidence catches about **76%** of such proteins, measured by holding out whole families, so **roughly one in four still arrives with no warning at all**. The limitation and the catch rate are both printed on every call, because a reader who reads silence as an all-clear would be wrong that often. (This entry previously said no threshold catches it. That was inferred from a single protein and is wrong: measured over ~1,500 unseen sequences, confidence reaches AUROC 0.941.)
- **Disorder prediction is weakest exactly where you need it most.** On sequences with no close relative in the PDB it currently underperforms not using a language model at all.
- **A predicted structure is not a structure.** An experimental temperature factor and an AlphaFold confidence occupy the same field of the same file format and mean opposite things. BOFFIN carries which one it has through the entire app and captions every predicted model accordingly.
- **Interaction analysis rests on assumptions about hydrogens.** Most crystal structures do not contain any, so hydrogen bonds are inferred from geometry alone. The specific assumptions travel *inside* the exported data, above the results, rather than as a footnote nobody reads.
- **The deposited coordinates are often not the molecule.** A dimer deposited as one chain looks like a monomer until the biological assembly is built. The viewer says which one you are looking at.

## 🔒 Everything stays on the device

There is no inference server, no account, and no telemetry. The privacy manifest declares that BOFFIN collects **nothing**, and not "nothing identifiable": nothing. Structure files can be fetched from the PDB and AlphaFold if you ask for them, and that is the only network traffic the app generates. An unpublished sequence pasted into BOFFIN never leaves the phone, which is the difference between using it on a real project and using it on a demo.

## ⚖️ Where the data comes from

Every model, dataset and library is recorded with its licence, read at source and dated, in `Docs/ATTRIBUTIONS.md`, and credited in the app whether or not the licence asks for it.

Two consequences worth stating plainly. **PLIP is GPL v2**, so BOFFIN's interaction analysis is a clean-room implementation of the published geometric criteria and PLIP's code is never linked, ported or consulted. And a licence question turned out to be a science question: the transmembrane predictor could not use the standard training sets because they are not redistributable, so the labels were rebuilt from **Swiss-Prot's own curated annotations**, which are CC BY 4.0. The replacement is not a compromise, it is where the curators put the annotation in the first place.

Where a source states no terms at all, it is used for non-commercial research and the position is recorded as a risk rather than dressed up as a permission (decision of 2026-08-25).

## 🔧 For developers

`BOFFIN.xcodeproj` is a plain Xcode project committed to the repository: clone it, open it, build it, with no generation step. The code sits in seven local Swift packages whose dependency graph is enforced by a script rather than by convention. Build, test and architecture detail is in [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md), the full engineering plan in `Docs/BOFFIN_BUILD_PLAN.md`, and every performance number in `Docs/perf-log.md`.

Test fixtures are real structures chosen so that each one can fail differently: ubiquitin, CDK2 with ATP, the β2-adrenergic receptor, PETase, α-synuclein by NMR, a 70S ribosome, a selenomethionine structure, and eight deliberately malformed inputs. Where scientific correctness is at stake, the test is written from the published definition before the implementation, because a numbering scheme that is silently wrong is worse than one that is missing: it will be believed.

- [x] **The machine learning core.** The premise proven: **98.8% of operations on the Neural Engine**, parity with the reference implementation on every length bucket, and a cross-language test so a tokenising mistake on the Swift side cannot hide behind its own consistency
- [x] **Secondary structure and disorder.** Trained from scratch, because the published heads cannot be adapted: they are built on a different, much larger backbone, their architectures would forfeit the Neural Engine entirely, and they are not redistributable. Measured against the published baselines *including* the no-language-model floor, which is the comparison that says whether the model is contributing anything
- [x] **Asked what the disorder benchmark actually says, before trying to beat it.** The plan was to improve the head on novel folds, where it measures below the no-language-model floor. That rested on one number: CASP12 MCC 0.500 against a floor of 0.573 — and **CASP12 has 21 chains**, whose 7,256 residues are not 7,256 independent observations, because disorder is contiguous and a forty-residue tail is one event. Bootstrapping by **chain** at the shipped threshold: CASP12 is **0.501, 95% interval [0.337, 0.662]**, which spans the floor. **The claim is not established**, and chasing it would have been optimising against noise on a benchmark that could not have confirmed the improvement. The deficit that *is* real is **CB513: 0.430, [0.372, 0.492], wholly below its floor of 0.502 across 513 chains** — the benchmark the project had been quoting least. TS115's "beats NetSurfP-2.0" also needs softening, with 12.8% of resamples below its own floor
- [x] **Tested solvent accessibility as an auxiliary task, and it does not help.** NetSurfP predicts RSA jointly, and RSA and disorder are arguably the same physical fact seen twice, so a shared trunk should give the rare binary label a denser signal. The labels were already on disk. Both arms used the shipped trunk, the same seed, bit-identical initialisation, the same batches in the same order, and thresholds tuned on the same validation chains and applied blind: **the control arm reproduces the shipped head** (CB513 0.426 against 0.430, and it independently tuned to the shipped 0.90 threshold), which is what makes the comparison worth reading. Result on CB513: **0.426 to 0.428, paired 95% interval [-0.007, +0.013]**. That tightness is the point: this is not "no effect detected" from an underpowered test, it is the effect **bounded below 0.013 MCC** against a deficit of 0.076. The avenue is closed rather than merely untried. Likely reason, offered as hypothesis: the embedding is frozen, so an auxiliary signal cannot reach the representation the way it does when NetSurfP trains its backbone
- [x] **Found the disorder comparison was against the wrong model, and corrected it.** The app said this head measures below a "no-language-model floor", implying the embeddings contribute nothing. **That floor is NetSurfP's architecture on one-hot input, not ours**, so the comparison changed the representation and the model at once and blamed the representation. Training **the same head** on one-hot amino acids, same seed, same chains, same schedule, with only the input projection resized: **CB513 0.380 against 0.426 with embeddings, and TS115 0.537 against 0.643**. The language model is worth **+0.046 and +0.106**, and the claim it added nothing was false. The real gap is to NetSurfP's *model and training budget*: our one-hot head gets 0.380 where theirs gets 0.502, and that 0.122 has nothing to do with embeddings. This also confirms the hypothesis offered after the RSA null, that a frozen embedding leaves the deficit in the head rather than the input
- [x] **Measured capacity, and it does not close the gap either.** Four arms, same seed and chains, paired over the same bootstrap draws. Doubling the width buys **+0.009 MCC with its interval's lower bound at zero, for 3.6x the parameters and 1.6 MB more asset**: an eighth of the deficit, on a head that must ship on a phone. A deeper head with a **61-residue receptive field against the shipped 29** does *not* help, and both changes together is the only arm that measurably **hurts**. That refutes the hypothesis the sweep was designed around, that disorder needs a larger neighbourhood than the head can see

The variants are handled because they were counted across all 521 human kinases rather than assumed
- [x] **Canonical numbering.** Alignment maps a pasted sequence onto the curated kinase-pocket and GPCR numbering tables, so the published position names land on *your* residues. Two corrections on the way: percent identity measured over aligned columns only is badly biased (ubiquitin scored 0.35 against a kinase pocket that way, on a short flattering overlap), and alignment score is the wrong instrument for deciding family membership, so numbering is gated on the motif detector instead
- [x] **The family classifier.** 97.8% top-1 across 100 families in under a megabyte. Confidence calibration was fitted and then deliberately *not* applied, because the held-out split said it made things worse: calibration is a correction for over-confidence, not a ritual
- [x] **Gave the classifier a way to know what it does not know.** The stated position was that no threshold catches an out-of-set protein, based on ubiquitin being called PF00076 at 79.7% while a centroid cosine failed to flag it. That was **one protein**, so it became an experiment: hold out whole *families* (20 of 100, five splits) and ask of ~1,500 genuinely unseen sequences whether any score rejects them without rejecting the ~1,000 known ones. Holding out families rather than sequences is load-bearing, since a sequence split leaves each held-out sequence's family in training and "unknown" would mean nothing. **Mahalanobis distance reaches AUROC 0.969 plus or minus 0.005**, against 0.945 for max softmax and **0.850 for the centroid cosine that was tried**: that method really was the wrong instrument, and the conclusion drawn from it was too broad. Mahalanobis is also far the most *stable* across splits, which matters more than the mean when shipping a threshold. At a 5% false-rejection rate it catches **80.5% of unseen families**. **The honest limit: one unseen protein in five is still missed**, so the closed-set caveat stays and the app now states the catch rate rather than letting silence read as an all-clear. Swift agrees with the Python that set the threshold, cross-checked on reference embeddings
- [x] **Expanded the classifier from 100 Pfam families to 500, and accuracy went *up*.** 40,158 sequences, single-label only. **Top-1 0.9858 against 0.9792**, with calibration error halved, on a task five times harder by construction: the larger per-family sample more than compensates. **And the rejection method reversed.** The trainer had been printing a detection rate measured on the *100-family* model, hardcoded. Re-measured at 500: Mahalanobis fell from AUROC 0.969 to 0.941 and from **0.805 to 0.736** at the operating point, while max softmax held flat at 0.941 and **0.763, and became four times more stable** (±0.009 against ±0.038). The original case for Mahalanobis was that stability matters more than the mean when shipping a threshold; applied consistently, that argument now selects max softmax, which also needs **no 1.88 MB asset at all**. The asset, its binary format, its loader and its parity test are removed. Nothing would have broken had this gone unchecked: the app would simply have shipped the worse score while quoting a rate belonging to a model it no longer runs
- [ ] **Expand further, or stop here.** Pfam has over 30,000 families. 500 covers far more of what a user will paste, and the evidence says coverage costs nothing in accuracy, but each step multiplies the embedding run
- [x] **Homolog search over the whole PDB.** Every protein in the archive reduced to one vector, searched exhaustively in 5.4 ms. Approximate search was considered and rejected: at this size the exact answer is already fast, so approximation would buy nothing and pay in recall you cannot see going missing. Three defects found on the way, none of which raised an error: a **truncated download** left 7% of the archive in the index and quietly redefined "the best structure of this protein" as "the alphabetically first one"; **ranking by resolution picks fragments**, so a 90-residue tail was about to represent a GPCR; and a length ceiling had silently excluded EGFR along with 3,533 others
- [x] **Whitened the search index, which was losing a quarter of its answers in silence.** Protein embeddings are anisotropic: two unrelated proteins already score 0.848 similar, so everything that distinguishes a real hit from a stranger lives in the last two percent of the range, which is precisely what compressing to 8 bits destroys. Recall was **0.748** with no error and no symptom. Removing the shared directions took it to **0.966**, and made the number on screen mean something as a side effect
- [x] **Construct design.** Truncations ranked against **hard constraints that are enforced rather than scored**, because a construct that bisects a catalytic motif is not a compromise, it is dead protein. Precedent from real PDB entries is translated into the user's own numbering. Tag planning refuses a protease whose recognition site occurs inside the construct, since TEV cutting the protein in half presents as proteolysis in the prep rather than as a design error
- [x] **The construct card.** Everything you would paste into a supplier's form, as plain text with a share sheet, and every number on it either measured or labelled a convention. One mismatch caught: the pH scale used was a parameter to the printer rather than a property of the result, so a card could print one provenance above the other scale's numbers
- [x] **Primer-ready DNA.** The codon usage table is **computed from 4,317 coding sequences and 1.34 million codons** of the *E. coli* reference genome rather than copied. The widely quoted published table for this organism rests on 14 genes: a fine sample of fourteen genes, and exactly the sort of artefact that acquires authority by being cited. A failing test bought one codon of lookahead, because a legal choice can strand the *next* residue into creating a restriction site
- [x] **Disulfide pairs as a hard constraint.** Measured from the coordinates rather than read from the deposited annotation, so it also works on a predicted model where there is nothing to read. Three things that look like details and are not: sulfur forms one bond, so pairs are assigned by shortest-first matching rather than by thresholding every contact (a crowded site puts three sulfurs inside the cutoff, and pairing one with two partners is not a close call, it is impossible); alternate locations are two models of one atom, so a cysteine modelled half bonded would otherwise bond to itself; and an NMR ensemble is twenty copies of the protein, so searching all models at once pairs a sulfur with its own image. Validated on **PETase, which has two disulfides at 2.046 and 2.052 A**, against CDK2, which has three cysteines and no bonds because the cytosol is reducing, and against ubiquitin, which has no cysteine at all. The test asserted one PETase bond and the finder found two: the finder was right, and writing the test from the published definition first works exactly as well when it is the test that is wrong. With no structure loaded the tab says disulfides were **not checked**, because a constraint list that silently omits them reads as a complete account of what may not be cut
- [x] **The structure viewer.** Mol\* vendored offline with a pinned version, structure files parsed from the specification, and any ruler track paintable onto the structure. **Ubiquitin loads with no network and Mol\* counts 660 atoms, exactly what BOFFIN's own parser counts**, which is two independent implementations agreeing. Nothing is ever interpolated into JavaScript, and a test proves it by passing a chain name that is an attempted injection and asserting it survives as data
- [x] **Fetching, picking, and predictions labelled as predictions.** Tap a residue and the app knows which one you meant, including in structures with more than one chain. PDB and AlphaFold fetch, with the AlphaFold model version pinned rather than "latest", because a silently changing model is a silently changing figure
- [x] **Biological assemblies and NMR ensembles.** The viewer lists what a structure declares, builds it, and says which you are looking at. Memory pressure releases the structure and keeps the page alive, so recovery is one command rather than a blank screen
- [x] **Figure export.** Mol\* renders the scene offscreen at an arbitrary size, so the figure that comes off a phone is the one that would come off a workstation: a screenshot of this screen is 1179 pixels across and no journal will take it. Sizes are named for what they are for (one column, two column, poster) rather than by pixel count, and transparent is the default, because a structure on an opaque white ground cannot be composited onto a coloured panel without cutting it out by hand and cutting out antialiased edges by hand is how a figure acquires a halo. **The size shown is read out of the PNG's own header, not taken from what was asked for**: those are two different claims and only one of them is in the file that goes to a journal. The test is shaped by the overlay that was built twice and removed twice, whose tests passed throughout because they asserted a command was *dispatched*; this one asserts the exported bytes really are a PNG of the requested resolution, which is only true if Mol\* really rendered it. - [x] **Alternate conformations, which were being resolved by accident.** A residue refined in two or three conformations is written as two or three copies of its atoms: alternative models of *one* residue, and every analysis walking the atom list has to choose. The secondary-structure assigner was choosing with `=`, so the **last** copy in file order won, and file order is altloc-code order, which has no relationship to occupancy. Measured on PETase: **25 residues carry alternate CA positions and the rule took the minor conformer in most of them**, including residue 53, where it picked the 0.29-occupancy copy over two at 0.35. Worse in principle than in that measurement, since nothing stopped the N coming from conformer A and the CA from conformer B: two different molecules, and a peptide built from both has bond lengths that exist in neither. Now one rule, shared: highest occupancy wins, ties to the alphabetically first code so two exports of one structure cannot disagree. The interaction profiler gets the same narrowing on both sides, since counting a doubled side chain twice reports two contacts where the crystallographer modelled one residue that cannot decide. `alt A` and `alt ''` are now selection keywords, case sensitively, because a file may use `a` and `A` for different conformations. **A second finding, in the test fixtures themselves**: the manifest credited 1E8A with exercising alternate locations and it has none, so anything written against it would have passed vacuously. PETase is the fixture that actually carries them, and the manifest now says so
- [x] **Symmetry mates.** The deposited coordinates are one molecule in a lattice, and a contact between two chains is either a biological interface or an artefact of how the crystal packed: those are indistinguishable without the neighbours, so this answers the question most often asked about an interface. **Whether an entry can have symmetry mates is read from the file's own `_cell` and `_symmetry`, not asked of the viewer** — Mol\* attaches symmetry as a lazily-computed model property, and reaching into that means depending on minified internals that have already caught this bridge out once. A zero cell is how a file says "not from a crystal", and both categories are frequently *present and empty* in NMR and predicted entries, so testing that the category exists would report every AlphaFold model as crystallographic. An entry with no lattice is told so as a fact rather than as a failure. Verified on ubiquitin, P 21 21 21, by asserting that neighbouring copies genuinely add atoms
- [x] **A PyMOL-style selection language.** Tokeniser, parser and evaluator. `byres (polymer within 5 of organic)` returns CDK2's ATP site, checked against structural facts rather than against the evaluator's own output. An unknown keyword names itself rather than quietly selecting everything or nothing, because an over-broad selection makes a figure that is wrong in a way nobody can see
- [x] **Measurement.** Distances, angles and torsions, with **the sign of a torsion treated as the point rather than a detail**: a viewer reporting the wrong sign is reporting the wrong conformation and both look equally plausible. Checked on constructed geometry and then on ubiquitin, where the peptide bond measures 1.33 Å and omega is trans. One hand-built test fixture was wrong, and the real-structure test passing beside it is what said so
- [x] **`.pml` round-tripping.** A scene deck built on a phone opens on a desktop. **The import is the half that has to fail loudly**, since a viewer that quietly ignores what it cannot represent draws a scene that is subtly not the one described. Selection expressions travel verbatim, which is the entire reason for adopting PyMOL's syntax rather than inventing one
- [x] **Scene decks and presentation mode.** Full-bleed presentation with presenter notes. **Advancing stops at the end rather than wrapping**, because finding slide one again reads as having lost your place, and it is deliberately not tap-to-advance: in front of a room you rotate the molecule far more often than you change slide
- [x] **Touch selection builder, so the language is optional.** `byres (polymer within 5 of organic)` is a fine thing to type on a keyboard and a miserable thing to type on a phone, where the brackets live two modifier taps away: a selection nobody can be bothered to type is a selection nobody makes. **The builder writes the language rather than replacing it** — every tap edits the same expression string a user could have typed, it stays visible and editable, and it is evaluated by the same parser as everything else. So it cannot express anything the language cannot, there is no second selection system to disagree with the first, and the expression still travels into a `.pml` file and opens on a desktop. The match count is live, and **an expression that parses and matches nothing is called out rather than shown as a number among numbers**: it is the failure that looks most like success and the one that puts a blank figure in front of someone. Parser errors now name the offending token, since "invalid selection" leaves a reader comparing their typing against a syntax they do not have
- [x] **Pencil annotation.** Explaining a structure to a room means pointing at it, and a laser pointer leaves no record: a Pencil circles the pocket and the mark is still there when the deck is sent on afterwards. Strokes are stored, not a bitmap, so an annotation redraws at the display's resolution and stays editable. **Anchored to the scene's own identity, not its index**: the build plan said index, and an index is not an anchor, since reordering or deleting renumbers everything after it and the drawing would slide silently onto a different slide and look perfectly deliberate on the wrong structure. That exposed a second defect: `ViewerScene.id` *was* its name, so two scenes called "Site" shared one identity. Identity is now stable and excluded from equality, which keeps `.pml` round-trips comparing by content. A performance trap on the way: `PKCanvasView` is a scroll-view subclass and its mere presence took an accessibility query from 24 seconds to a timeout, reading as a broken deck rather than a busy tree, so the live canvas exists only while the pen is up and the strokes render as an image otherwise
- [ ] **External display.** A presenter view on the device with the structure on the projector. Needs real hardware to develop against and to verify honestly
- [x] **Interaction analysis, clean-room.** Hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, stacking, hydrophobic contacts and metal coordination from the published geometric criteria, in one tunable set of cutoffs. **Where a criterion cannot honestly be applied it is not applied silently.** One test tightens every cutoff and asserts strictly fewer contacts, because a criterion that is read and never used looks exactly like one that is used and generous
- [x] **Interaction diagram, summary and export.** Drawn on a **circle rather than a force-directed layout**, which would look more like the published figures and would move a residue between two renders of the same site, making the figures impossible to compare. Contact types differ by dash pattern as well as colour so the figure survives being printed in grey, and VoiceOver reads every one
- [x] **Fixed two viewer commands that were doing nothing at all.** The vendored build exports far fewer names at the top level than its source suggests, and code written against the missing ones does not throw: it evaluates to undefined and takes the fallback branch. Assembly listing returned empty forever and every pick reported the first atom rather than the one tapped, always plausibly. Every test passed throughout, because they checked a command was *dispatched* rather than that it did anything
- [ ] **Verify assembly discovery against a structure known to declare one.** Implemented and guarded, but the test asserting it was deleted rather than pinned to one viewer version
- [x] **The 3D interaction overlay, and the defect that broke it twice.** Built and removed twice before, both times diagnosed as a selection-language problem, and **the selection language was innocent**. The real cause: loading a structure *adds* it to Mol\*'s hierarchy rather than replacing what is there, while every read in the bridge indexes the first structure ever loaded. So the viewer showed the new molecule and answered every query about the old one, and endpoints computed on a kinase were resolved against ubiquitin. Of course nothing matched. The same defect meant the atom count reported after a second load was the *previous* structure's, which nothing had caught because the only count ever checked was small enough to look plausible. Endpoints are now named by chain, residue and atom rather than by index, so there is no index for two implementations to disagree about, and the drawn-count is on screen rather than in a log. **55 of 55 contacts now reach the structure.** The replacement behaviour has its own regression test, on the atom count rather than through the overlay, because a bug this general should not be guarded only by the feature that happened to expose it
- [x] **Opening files and Shortcuts.** A sequence shared from Mail, Files or AirDrop opens and analyses. Two Shortcuts actions run **without bringing the app forward**, and expose only the analysis that needs no model, because a Shortcut that sometimes fails because an asset was not downloaded is worse than one that never offered
- [ ] **Extensions and sync.** Share extension, Quick Look preview, CloudKit sync, Handoff, widget and Live Activity
- [x] **Audio Graphs.** Continuous tracks carry a chart description, so a VoiceOver user **hears** hydropathy or a mutation-tolerance profile rather than being read a summary of it. A track is a shape, and the question a shape answers is *where does it change*. Gaps stay gaps: only continuous tracks are sonified, because a tone where there is no measurement is a lie in a new medium
- [x] **Privacy manifest and acknowledgements.** The manifest declares that BOFFIN collects nothing. Every source is credited whether or not its licence asks, and the terms column reads "None stated" in orange where that is true, because rounding an unstated licence up on an acknowledgements screen would mislead exactly where a reader has gone looking for the truth
- [x] **Answered whether the dataset curation was load-bearing.** Heads trained on labels taken **straight from PDB entries** (5,012 chains, 1.48 million residues, split so the score cannot be memorisation) land inside the range of the heads trained on the curated versions of the same underlying data. Not a like-for-like comparison and not quoted as one. The licence question, since decided separately, was never a question about quality
- [x] **Onboarding, and empty states that act.** A one-time welcome sheet saying the three things a new user needs, in that order: nothing leaves the device, one pass gives four answers, and what it gets wrong. The third is the point, because a welcome screen that only sells is how someone comes to trust the closed-set classifier the first time it is confidently wrong. Three tabs previously said "load a sequence in the Order tab first" and offered no way to do it, which is a dead end wearing the clothes of an explanation; each now carries the input sheet and both examples. The suppression flag the UI tests use has its own guard: one test deliberately launches without it, since fourteen green tests would say nothing about whether onboarding exists at all
- [x] **Dynamic Type audit.** Ten fixed point sizes, classified on a distinction a protein app has to make: text laid out as text must scale, text drawn inside a canvas must not, because a residue label has to fit the column it labels and enlarging it to 310% makes the heat map unreadable rather than accessible. Nine were text and are fixed, all of them 9 or 10 points and so below the legibility floor even at the default setting. One defect fell out: `Typography.sequence` claimed in its own doc comment to scale with Dynamic Type and never did, while the test checked only the floor, so nothing could contradict the half that did not exist
- [x] **Error states, and the conflation underneath them.** Five places handed the user a raw Core ML error string, which is a good diagnostic and not a message. Failures now carry what did not happen, what to do about it, and the raw text kept verbatim behind a disclosure. Cancellation is never reported as a failure, being offline says what still works, and recovery advice is absent rather than invented. Underneath, three state enums each had one case carrying both "this asset was never downloaded" and "this operation failed", so a corrupt index displayed as a download in progress
- [ ] **TestFlight.** Needs the Apple Developer account, signing identities and an App Store Connect record
- [ ] **Native renderer and augmented reality.** A Metal renderer benchmarked against Mol\* rather than assumed to beat it, and RealityKit presentation. Gated on measured performance, not preference
- [ ] **Choose a licence.** Constrained by the copyleft dependency that is already avoided by clean-room implementation, and by what an App Store release permits
- [ ] **Confirm three fixture caveats.** Whether the GPCR tests should assert against the fusion construct or the canonical sequence, whether the interaction profiler wants a drug-like inhibitor rather than ATP, and whether the ribosome should be fetched on demand to lighten the repository

## 🚫 Out of scope for v1

Structure prediction (no AlphaFold or Boltz running on the device), multiple sequence alignment generation, molecular dynamics, docking, and any cloud inference whatsoever. Prediction requests are handed off to [BoltzMaker](https://github.com/bellcheddar/BoltzMaker) on the desktop instead.
